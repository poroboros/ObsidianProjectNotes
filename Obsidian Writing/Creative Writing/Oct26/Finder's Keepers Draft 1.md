Glint overlooked the forest of corn from his roost. He could still hear the beast wailing, and as hungry as he was, he wasn't going to risk crossing paths with one of them. Glint let out an exasperated click before diving from the branch. Despite doing this for as long as he could remember, Glint still loved how with wind felt on his body, how its gentle roar filled his ears as he approached the ground. With a last minute flick of his wings, he touched the ground without even disturbing a single spec of dirt. It took him a good moment to realize that it was too quiet: no chirping, no rustling, and certainly no howling. He knew there were several beasts posted around and through the forest, but from what he had gathered, if the one he had seen before was gone, he liked his odds from this approach. With a clumsy little hop, he took flight towards the cornfield, staying as low as possible to avoid the ire of any beasts.

As he drew nearer a new sound came to him, inaudible at first but the growing into a caw clear as the dawn itself. "Help! Help! Where am I? I can't see anything!" Glint landed atop a stalk of corn to see a strange wriggling mass before him. It appeared to be a beast hide forming a vibrant red and black array. "Please! Steven!" There was no doubt, the call was coming from beneath the hide. Before he could realize what he was doing, Glint was already on the hide, plucking off the shiny bits holding it together. 

"Are you okay?" Glint cried as he wrested the other from beneath the hide. 

"Yeah, I uh, uh..." the other gasped out. Glint examined him as he gathered his breath. Not only was he a crow like him, but he was quite the specimen, with short and prim tail-feathers and a coloration that seemed to sparkle in the morning light. Glint evaded the other bird's gaze as it tried to meet his, mortified by his blatant gawking.  Before Glint could think of something clever to say to break the awkwardness the other stumbled back with a yelp. "You... you're a bird!" Glint embarrassment melted away at the inanity of the statement; he really must of been struggling for air under there. 

"Yes? Are you okay?" Glint repeated, "It really isn't safe to stay here for too long, they could come back any minute." The other bird did not respond, his breath turning to a violent wheeze that shook his whole body. Instinctively, Glint wrapped his wing around the other. "Hey hey, its going to be okay. I didn't get your name?" After a short pause in breath the other responded 
"H.. Harvey". 

Harvey? For such a strange name it was weirdly familiar. "Okay Harvey, I need you to name five thing you can see." 

"Uhh... I see corn, the sky, trees, dirt... you?" 

"Good Harvey, now tell me four things you can feel." Glint continued the mantra, the words coming from somewhere within him he couldn't place. 

"Um, I feel the ground, the wind, the sun. I... I feel feathers on my..." Harvey began to breath heavily again. "This can't be real, I... I just touched the scarecrow and..." Glint felt like he was choking on the unease in his throat. Something was wrong about this bird. Yet, he couldn't leave him in this state. 

"Very good Harvey." Glint continued with false confidence. "Next I need three things you can hear". Harvey inhaled deeply before cawing again. "I hear corn rustling. I hear birds singing..." Harvey paused, as if the crude flirts of the blue-jays wasn't something they heard everyday. Perhaps he flustered easily. 

"I hear..." Before Harvey could finish a crack of thunder crossed the field. Glint was off the ground before he even realized. 

"I told you we needed to... " Harvey was petrified on the ground, blood dripping from his left wing, the beast not even a shadow away from him. It nearly towered over the cornstalks an outer hide of unnatural colors covering its brush-like tangle of fur. It bared its yellow fangs as it slowly approached carrying a large stick dappled with shiny specks. Harvey scarcely had time to yelp in pain before Glint was dragging him by the leg through the rows of corn. Glint winced as he pulled Harvey along the ground, unable to lift him any further while still being in flight. For now, he just had to make sure the two of them were somewhere safe.

---

Glint tried to set Harvey down gently, though he worried it hardly made a difference at this point. He looked dreadful, with dirt and blood matting his feathers in a vile maroon sludge. The only conciliation he could muster was that no one had yet found this stash. It held his most valuable trinket and he was certain that it would have been taken by now if it was known.  

"I smell blood and pine." Harvey croaked out. "I... I think my dad said that pine sap was disinfectant?" He tilted his head towards a tree knob with considerable labor. "C-Can you put it on my wing?" He cawed, as if he couldn't believe what was leaving his beak. Glint obeyed instantaneously, not even pausing to consider the words he knew he shouldn't know the meaning of. Glint dutifully washed the blood from his once beautiful feathers before applying the sap, his gut at once feeling like it was full of pebbles and thrumming with a strangely familiar warmth. Glint suppressed the feelings; Harvey was in real danger and he had to focus on their survival. As if on cue, a pang of hunger struck him. He had been so distracted by everything that happened he forgot to grab a single morsel of corn. "I... I'm scared. This isn't real, I need to find him." Harvey gasped out. Glint hurried to comfort him.

"It's okay, I'm here with you Harvey." The other did not respond, his rib cage weakly rising and falling as his eyes shut. Glint counted his heartbeats, watching to make sure they were not coming to a stop. After some time, he resolved himself. Neither of them were going to be okay if they starved. The sun was setting and he didn't have time to forage aimlessly. He had to return to the cornfield.


---

Night had fallen by the time Glint arrived at the cornfield. Landing atop a cornstalk, he wasted no time in picking away the husk. Upon breaking through, he ate as much as his stomach would hold before taking pieces into his talons to return to Harvey. It was then that he took notice of where he was, the ruffled feathers and discarded hide marking it as where the two of them had met. Something shiny caught in soft yellow moonlight and his mounting dread was supplanted by irresistible curiosity. Glint gingerly landed near the hide and pulled the trinket free. It was a vibrant pink rock with a long spider-like thread through it. Glint's breath caught. It was no common stone, not one smoothed by the river, or even a glittering crystal. It was shaped like  an ivy leaf cut in half but with none of the notches or imperfections. It has precise, hard, unnatural, and exactly like his. All of him felt heavy as he took in the trinket before, realizing far too late that it was no longer reflecting moonlight.

Before he could even unfurl his wings, a pale claw wrapped around his body, tightly pinning his wing to his body. With a high mocking howl, the beast hoisted him, corn slipping from his grip. It held him no more than a wing's length away from his face, continuing its rhythmic cry as it examined him. Whatever bravery Harvey had inspired him evacuated his body when the beast revealed its crooked yellow fangs.  As he closed his eyes and prepared for the worst, he felt its grip loosen enough to alleviate the pain, but not enough to wriggle free. One by one the feathers on his neck prickled as its stroked its claws up and down his throat, each of them waiting for the claws to quit pretending and wring the life out of him. When Glint opened his eyes again, he was falling, wings flopping free as he barely kept from hitting the ground. Glint falpped harder than he ever had before, wings burning as they carried him from certain death.

---

Harvey was beginning to stir when Glint returned. Though it was cleaned, his wing was still a ghastly site, like the last leaf standing in an autumn rainstorm.  Glint gulped as he prepared to deliver news of his failure.

"D-Did you get that for me?" Harvey's head gestured to Glint's foot, where a kernel of corn remained. Hadn't he dropped them all?

"This was all I could escape with. I swear, the beast almost ripped my head clean off." Harvey tilted his head quizzitively, before pawing the kernel towards himself.

"I don't think I ever got you name." Harvey cawed quietly.

"Glint. Chose it myself." Glint responsed as Harvey pecked away at the Kernel.

"Gkint, I-I need to tell you something. I'm really not a..." Harvey trailed off.

"Are you okay?" The other bird stared back blanky.

"Um, yeah I think its getting better. Glint right?"

"Yes, I think so too. Let me dress it again" Glint cawed as he spread a globule of sap on the other's wing.

"Where did you learn to do that?" The other replied.

"Your dad showed us when he took us camping, Harvey. Said you could use it as a disinfectant in a pinch." Glint remembered at last.
